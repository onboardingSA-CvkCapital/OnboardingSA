Transnet is the state-owned company that moves much of South Africa's freight. It runs the national freight rail network, manages the country's commercial ports, operates the pipelines that carry fuel and gas inland, and maintains and builds rolling stock. Together, that makes it one of the biggest employers of technical, operational and engineering staff in the country, as well as a large employer of people in finance, HR, procurement, IT and legal functions.

Transnet advertises its jobs on its own careers site, the Transnet Talent Portal. The adverts are detailed and follow a consistent format, which makes them easy to read once you understand the structure. This guide explains the divisions, how to read an advert, how the portal works, and how to prepare an application that has a real chance of being shortlisted.

The Transnet operating divisions

Every Transnet advert names an operating division. Each division has its own operations, locations and types of jobs, so the division tells you a lot about where you would work and what kind of environment it is.

Freight railRuns freight trains on the national rail network.Train drivers, yard staff, rail engineers, signalling and maintenance technicians.
National Ports AuthorityManages port infrastructure and marine services.Marine pilots, tug and marine engineers, port planners, harbour masters.

There are also group-level functions, such as corporate finance, legal, group HR and strategy, which are usually based in Johannesburg. Port and terminal jobs are concentrated in port cities such as Durban, Richards Bay, Cape Town, Saldanha, Gqeberha, Ngqura and East London, while rail and engineering jobs are spread across the country.

How to read a Transnet advert

Transnet adverts usually start with a short table of key facts. Understanding each line saves time and helps you judge quickly whether a post is right for you.

  • Operating Division: which part of Transnet the job is in.
  • Employee Group: whether the post is permanent, fixed-term or part of a specific programme.
  • Department: the function within the division, such as People Management, Finance or Engineering.
  • Location: the town or site where you would work.
  • Reporting To: the title of your manager, which gives you a sense of where the post sits.
  • Grade: a letter that places the post in Transnet's grading structure. Salaries are usually not published, so the grade is the best guide to the level.
  • Reference: the requisition number, such as "req6113". Keep a note of it for every post you apply for.

After the key facts, the advert normally states the closing date, with a reminder that it is the applicant's responsibility to make sure HR receives the application before that date. Then come the main sections: Position Purpose (what the job is for), Position Outputs (the duties), Qualifications and Experience, Competencies, and an Equity Statement.

Qualifications and experience

This section lists what you must have, and sometimes what would be an advantage. Transnet adverts are specific: they might ask for a three-year degree in a named field, a number of years of relevant experience, a driver's licence of a certain code, and occasionally the ability to travel between regions. Read it as a checklist. If a requirement is described as compulsory, assume shortlisting will check for it.

Competencies

The competencies section lists the knowledge and skills the job needs, such as knowledge of labour legislation for an employee relations post, or fault-finding skills for a technician post. These are the areas you will be assessed on at interview, so they are your best preparation guide.

Equity statementsMost Transnet adverts say that preference will be given to suitably qualified applicants from designated groups in line with the employment equity plan of the division. Some posts specifically prefer persons with disabilities. This is about preference in selection, not a ban on other applicants. If you meet the requirements, you can apply.

Applying on the Transnet Talent Portal

The Talent Portal is where you build your profile and apply. The process is similar to other large company portals.

  1. Create your profile. Register with a personal email address you check regularly. Avoid shared or work emails that you might lose access to.
  2. Complete your details fully. Fill in education, work history and skills completely and honestly, with dates. Many applications are screened against requirements before a person reads them.
  3. Upload a strong CV and your documents. Your CV should show clearly how you meet the requirements of the kind of posts you want. Upload your ID and qualifications if the portal asks for them.
  4. Search and read carefully. Use the job search to find posts by keyword or location, and read the full advert before applying.
  5. Apply for each post separately. Each requisition is a separate process, so apply for every suitable post individually.
  6. Apply early. Do not wait for the closing date. Technical problems on the last day are your risk, not Transnet's.

Preparing for selection and interviews

Shortlisted candidates are usually contacted for assessments and interviews. Depending on the post, you may face psychometric tests, technical or practical assessments, medical examinations for operational and safety-critical roles, and panel interviews. Transnet's work is heavily regulated and safety-critical in many areas, so expect questions about safety, compliance and procedure, even for posts that are not purely technical.

Use the advert as your study guide. For each item under Position Outputs, think of a time you did something similar and what the result was. For each competency, be ready to explain how you apply it in practice. If the advert mentions specific legislation, systems or standards, refresh your knowledge of them before the interview.

Appointments are usually subject to checks, which can include qualification verification, criminal record checks and, for some posts, credit checks and medical fitness. Be honest in your application and in any declarations: false information can lead to an offer being withdrawn.

Routes for young people

Transnet has long been a major trainer of young South Africans. Its programmes have included engineering and technical bursaries, graduate development programmes, apprenticeships and learnerships through its training facilities, and training for specialist operational roles such as train drivers and marine officers. Not every programme runs every year, so watch the Talent Portal and Transnet's official announcements for current intakes. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Applying with an incomplete profile, or a CV that does not show dates and duties clearly.
  • Ignoring the location. Some posts require you to live near a specific port, depot or plant.
  • Missing a compulsory requirement such as the driver's licence code or a professional registration.
  • Waiting for the closing date and running into technical problems.
  • Responding to "Transnet" job offers on social media that ask for money or personal documents outside the official portal.
