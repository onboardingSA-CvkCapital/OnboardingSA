How to Claim UIF After Losing Your Job

If you have lost your job, UIF can give you an income while you look for the next one. Here is how to check whether you qualify and how to claim without delays.

In short

  • Most employees contribute 1% of their pay to UIF, and employers add another 1%.
  • You build up one credit day for every four days you worked and contributed, up to a maximum of 365 days in four years.
  • Benefits are a percentage of your previous pay, usually between about 38% and 60%, and lower for longer claims.
  • Apply within 12 months of losing your job, online through uFiling or at a Department of Employment and Labour office.

Losing a job is one of the most stressful experiences in anyone's life. Bills do not stop arriving, and finding the next job can take months. The Unemployment Insurance Fund (UIF) exists for exactly this situation. If you were employed and contributing to UIF, you may be able to receive part of your previous income while you look for work.

Many people who qualify never claim, or give up because of delays. This guide explains, in plain language, who qualifies, how much you could get, which documents you need and how to apply, so that your claim goes through as smoothly as possible.

What UIF is

The Unemployment Insurance Fund is a fund run by the Department of Employment and Labour. Most employees and their employers contribute to it every month:

  • The employee contributes 1% of their pay.
  • The employer contributes another 1%.

The contribution is calculated up to an earnings ceiling, so very high earners do not contribute on their full salary. Your payslip should show the UIF deduction every month.

UIF pays several types of benefit: unemployment benefits, illness benefits, maternity and parental benefits, adoption benefits and death benefits for dependants. This guide focuses on unemployment benefits, which you claim after losing your job.

Who qualifies for unemployment benefits

You may qualify if:

  • You were employed and you and your employer contributed to UIF.
  • You lost your job through no choice of your own, for example through dismissal, retrenchment, the end of a fixed-term contract, or your employer's insolvency.
  • You are now unemployed and available for work.
  • You apply within the time limit, which is generally 12 months after you stopped working.

You generally do not qualify for unemployment benefits if you resigned voluntarily, if you are still employed, or if you were never registered and never contributed. Some workers, such as certain categories of government employees and people on specific types of contracts, are not covered by UIF. If you are not sure about your situation, ask at a labour centre.

Check that your employer declared youYour claim depends on your employer having registered you and declared your earnings to UIF. If your payslip showed UIF deductions but the department has no record of you, the employer may not have paid them over. This is the employer's responsibility, and the department can follow up, but it may delay your claim. Keep all your payslips.

How much you can receive

Credit days

UIF works on credit days. For every four days you worked and contributed, you earn one credit day. The maximum you can build up is 365 credit days in a four-year period. Each day you claim uses up one credit day.

So, as a rough guide, if you worked and contributed for four years without claiming, you could have up to 365 credit days. If you worked for one year, you would have about 91 credit days.

The benefit amount

Your benefit is a percentage of your average daily pay before you became unemployed, calculated on a sliding scale:

  • Lower earners receive a higher percentage of their previous pay, up to about 60%.
  • Higher earners receive a lower percentage, down to about 38%, and the calculation uses the earnings ceiling.
  • For the first 238 days of a claim, benefits are paid at this rate (between about 38% and 60%).
  • For credit days after the first 238, the rate drops to 20%.

The department calculates the exact amount based on your declared earnings. It will not replace your full salary, but it can make a real difference while you look for work.

Documents you need

Gather these before you apply:

  1. Your ID (or passport and work permit for foreign nationals who qualify).
  2. Your last six payslips, if you have them.
  3. The UI-19 form completed by your employer. This is the employer's declaration that you have stopped working, giving your dates of employment, earnings and the reason employment ended.
  4. A certificate of service from your employer, if available.
  5. Proof of banking details, such as a bank-stamped confirmation letter or a recent bank statement in your name.
  6. Any documents about your dismissal or retrenchment, such as a dismissal letter or retrenchment notice.

Your former employer is required to give you the UI-19 form. If they refuse or delay, tell the labour centre. The department can follow up with the employer, and you should not have to wait indefinitely.

How to apply

Online through uFiling

The Department of Employment and Labour runs an online system called uFiling. You register as a user, verify your details, and then submit an application for unemployment benefits and upload your documents. You can also track your claim and submit continuation forms online. Make sure you use the official government uFiling site, and never enter your details on a link sent to you by a stranger.

At a labour centre

You can also apply in person at a Department of Employment and Labour office, often called a labour centre. Take your original ID and all your documents, and copies. Go early, as queues can be long, especially at the start of the month.

Whichever way you apply, keep a record of your reference number and the date you submitted.

After you apply

  • Assessment. The department checks your details, your employer's declarations and your credit days.
  • Payment. If approved, payments are made into your bank account in instalments.
  • Continuation. You may need to confirm regularly that you are still unemployed, either online or at a labour centre, to keep receiving payments.
  • Job search support. The department may register you on its employment services database and refer you to opportunities or training.

As soon as you start a new job, stop claiming and tell the department. Continuing to claim while employed is fraud and can lead to repayment demands and prosecution.

Common reasons claims are delayed

  1. The employer did not declare you or did not pay over contributions.
  2. A missing or incomplete UI-19 form.
  3. Incorrect banking details, or an account in someone else's name.
  4. Mismatched personal details, such as a name spelled differently from your ID.
  5. Applying too late, after the 12-month limit.
  6. Not submitting continuation forms when required.

Beware of UIF scams

Because many people are desperate for help with UIF, scammers offer to "fast-track" claims for a fee, or send fake links asking for your ID number, banking details or uFiling password. Claiming UIF is free, and you do not need an agent. Never share your banking PIN or online banking password, and only use official government channels. Looking for your next job

UIF gives you some breathing room, so use the time well. Update your CV, apply widely and consider learnerships or training that can strengthen your next application. Our CV guide and cover letter guide can help, and you can search current vacancies from official sources on our homepage.

Questions people ask

Can I claim UIF if I resigned?

Generally, unemployment benefits are for people who lost their job through no choice of their own, such as dismissal, retrenchment or the end of a fixed-term contract. If you resigned, you usually do not qualify for unemployment benefits, although there are exceptions and you can ask at a labour centre about your situation.

How long does UIF take to pay?

It depends on how complete your documents are and how busy the office is. Claims with all documents, correct banking details and an employer who has declared you properly are processed fastest. Missing employer information is the most common reason for delays.

Do I have to pay someone to claim UIF for me?

No. Claiming UIF is free. You can apply yourself online or at a labour centre. Be wary of anyone who asks for a fee or for your banking login details to 'speed up' your claim.

Can I claim UIF while I look for work?

Yes, that is what unemployment benefits are for. You may be asked to confirm regularly that you are still unemployed, and you must stop claiming and inform the department once you start a new job.
