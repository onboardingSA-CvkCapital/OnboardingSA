Learnerships vs Internships vs Apprenticeships

They sound similar, but they're not the same — and choosing the right one can shape your whole career. Here's what each means in South Africa.

Career Guides · OnboardingSA

If you're young and looking for your first real step into work, you'll see three words over and over: learnership, internship, and apprenticeship. They all mix work with learning, and they can all lead to a permanent job — but they work differently, pay differently, and suit different people. This guide breaks down each one clearly.

Learnerships

A learnership is a structured programme that combines classroom learning with practical work experience, and it leads to a registered qualification on the National Qualifications Framework (NQF). Learnerships are governed by the Skills Development Act and run through the various SETAs (Sector Education and Training Authorities).

  • Who it's for: Both unemployed young people and existing employees who want a formal qualification.
  • How long: Usually 12 months.
  • Pay: Learners receive a monthly stipend (an allowance), not a full salary. The amount varies by employer and SETA.
  • The outcome: A recognised NQF qualification plus real work experience — a strong combination on any CV.
Good to know: Because a learnership ends in a formal qualification, it's especially valuable if you didn't get to study further after matric. You earn while you learn, and you come out with a certificate that employers recognise.

Internships

An internship gives you practical work experience in your field, usually after you've already completed a qualification (or while you're studying). It's about applying what you've learned in a real workplace and building experience that makes you employable.

  • Who it's for: Graduates and students who have the theory but need workplace experience.
  • How long: Commonly 6 to 24 months.
  • Pay: Usually a stipend. Some internships pay well; others pay little — check before you commit.
  • The outcome: Experience, references, and often a foot in the door for a permanent role at the same organisation.

Apprenticeships

An apprenticeship trains you in a skilled trade — electrician, boilermaker, fitter and turner, welder, motor mechanic, plumber. It combines on-the-job training with technical education, and it leads to a trade test. Pass the trade test and you become a qualified artisan.

  • Who it's for: People who want a hands-on technical career in a trade.
  • How long: Typically 3 to 4 years.
  • Pay: Apprentices earn an allowance that usually increases as they progress.
  • The outcome: A nationally recognised trade qualification (Red Seal) — artisans are in high demand and can earn very well.

Quick comparison

  • Want a formal qualification while working, even without further study? Learnership.
  • Already qualified and need workplace experience? Internship.
  • Want a hands-on trade and long-term earning power? Apprenticeship.
They're all real jobs to take seriously. Show up on time, work hard, ask questions, and treat the people around you well. Many permanent jobs in South Africa start exactly this way — the programme is your audition.

How to find and apply

Large employers, government departments, and SETAs advertise these programmes regularly, often early in the year. Watch for closing dates — they're strict. You'll usually need your matric certificate, your ID, and a short CV. For learnerships and apprenticeships, some programmes prioritise applicants who haven't done one before, so apply while you're eligible.
