Getting a Retail Job at Pick n Pay, TFG and Other Big Stores

Retail is one of the biggest sources of entry-level jobs in South Africa, and also one of the best places to build a career from the shop floor up. Here is how store hiring works and how to stand out.

In short

  • Large retailers now recruit mostly through online careers sites, not CVs dropped at the store.
  • Hours in titles matter: '45hr' usually means a full-time weekly contract, while '120hr' usually means a flexi contract of about 120 hours a month.
  • Store adverts often require the area you live in to be close to the store.
  • Customer service, reliability and honesty matter more than experience for entry-level posts.

Supermarkets, clothing stores and sports shops employ hundreds of thousands of South Africans. For many people, a retail job is a first job: a way to earn, gain experience and build the reference that opens the next door. For others it becomes a full career, from sales associate to supervisor, department manager, store manager and beyond. Groups such as Pick n Pay, TFG (which runs brands like Foschini, Markham, Totalsports and Sportscene) and other national chains are constantly hiring, especially before peak seasons.

Retail hiring has changed. Ten years ago you might have walked in with a CV and asked for the manager. Today most big retailers recruit through online careers sites, and store managers choose from the applicants who come through that system. This guide explains how it works now, what common job titles mean, and how to make your application stand out.

How the big retailers recruit

National retailers typically use an online recruitment system for both store and head office jobs. You search for vacancies, create a profile and apply for each post. The system then passes applications to the store or regional recruiter, who shortlists candidates for interviews.

  • Pick n Pay advertises store, distribution and head office posts on its online careers site, including store management, department managers, butchery and bakery posts, and supply chain roles.
  • TFG advertises store manager, assistant manager and sales associate posts for its many brands, as well as head office and distribution roles, on its own careers site.
  • Other retailers follow similar patterns, each with its own careers portal.

Some retailers also post "register your interest" or talent pool adverts, which collect applications for future openings rather than one specific job. These can be worth applying for, but they are not the same as an open vacancy, so do not stop applying for specific posts while you wait.

Reading a retail job title

Retail job titles can contain a lot of information in a few words. A typical title might look like this: "Sales Associate (120hr) - Totalsports - Somerset Mall". Breaking it down:

  • The role: Sales Associate.
  • The hours: the number in brackets. In many retail adverts, a number such as 45hr describes a full-time post with a weekly hours contract, while numbers such as 120hr or the word Flexi describe a contract with fewer guaranteed hours, often counted per month. The details are in the advert and contract.
  • The brand: Totalsports, one of the group's brands.
  • The store: Somerset Mall, which tells you exactly where you would work.

Store adverts may also add the area or province. Pay attention to the location: many retailers prefer, and sometimes require, candidates who live near the store, because shifts start early and finish late and transport costs matter.

Check the hours before you acceptA flexi or part-time contract can be a good way in, but it may not give you a full month's pay. Before accepting an offer, make sure you understand how many hours you are guaranteed, how shifts are allocated, and how you will be paid.

Many entry-level adverts ask for Grade 10 or Grade 12, basic computer skills and good communication in English. Some ask for previous retail or customer service experience; others welcome first-time job seekers. Read the requirements, and do not rule yourself out if you meet the minimum but have little experience.

Supervisor and management roles

If you already have retail experience, the next steps are supervisor, department manager (such as bakery, butchery or deli manager), assistant store manager and store manager. These adverts usually ask for:

  • A number of years of retail experience, often including some supervisory experience.
  • Specific department knowledge, such as butchery, fresh produce or bakery operations, for department manager roles.
  • Experience with stock control, shrinkage (stock losses), budgets and sales targets.
  • People management: rosters, training, performance and discipline.
  • Sometimes a driver's licence, especially for area or regional roles.

Retailers also run management trainee and graduate programmes for people with post-matric qualifications, which can lead to store management or head office careers. Our guide to graduate programmes explains how these generally work.

How to make your application stand out

  1. Complete your profile fully. Fill in every section of the online form, including your availability. Many systems filter by availability and location.
  2. Write a short, relevant CV. Even with no formal experience, you can show responsibility: helping in a family business, volunteering, sports, church or school leadership. Our first job CV guide shows how.
  3. Show you understand customer service. In your profile summary, mention reliability, honesty and helping customers. These are the qualities store managers say matter most.
  4. Apply for stores you can realistically reach. Early starts and late finishes mean location matters. Applying to stores near you shows you have thought about it.
  5. Apply early in the season. Retailers often hire ahead of busy periods such as the festive season and back-to-school.

Interviews for store jobs

Store interviews are often short and practical. A store manager or department manager may ask you about your availability, why you want to work in retail, how you would handle an unhappy customer, what you would do if you saw a colleague stealing, and how you work under pressure. Some retailers also use short online assessments or numeracy tests.

Prepare by visiting the store beforehand: notice how staff greet customers, how the store is laid out and what the brand sells. Dress neatly, arrive early and bring a copy of your ID and CV. Our first interview guide covers the basics, and honesty about your availability is always better than promising hours you cannot work.

Building a career from the shop floor

Retail rewards people who are reliable, learn quickly and take responsibility. Once you are in, ask about internal training, learn every section of the store, volunteer for stock counts and promotions, and show that you can be trusted with keys, cash and people. Many retailers advertise supervisor and management posts internally first, so a strong track record in one store can lead to promotion in another.

Questions people ask

Can I still hand in my CV at the store?

Some stores will accept a CV, but most big retailers now require you to apply through their online careers site, and store managers often cannot process paper CVs. Apply online first, and only hand in a CV if the store manager or the advert invites you to.

What does 'Flexi' or '120hr' mean in a store job title?

These usually describe contracts with fewer guaranteed hours than a full-time post, often worked in shifts across the week and weekends. The number usually refers to hours per month. The advert or the contract explains the exact hours, so check before you accept.

Do retailers do credit and criminal checks?

Many do, especially for posts that handle cash, stock or customer accounts. Adverts often say that appointments are subject to background checks. Honesty on your application matters more than a perfect record.

Is retail a dead-end job?

Not at all. Many store managers, area managers and head office staff started as cashiers, packers or sales associates. Retailers run training and management development programmes, and reliable staff are often promoted from within.
