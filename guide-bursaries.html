How to Apply for Bursaries in South Africa

A bursary can pay for your studies and set up your career — but the good ones are competitive and the deadlines are strict. Here's how to give yourself the best chance.

Career Guides · OnboardingSA

Studying in South Africa is expensive, and a bursary can change everything — covering your tuition, and sometimes accommodation, books and a living allowance too. Many bursaries also come with a job at the end. But competition is fierce, and every year strong candidates miss out simply because they applied late or left something out. This guide shows you how to do it properly.

What is a bursary?

A bursary is financial support for your studies that you usually don't pay back — unlike a student loan. In return, many bursaries expect you to work for the sponsor for a set period after you graduate (often one year for each year they funded you). This "work-back" arrangement is common with company and government bursaries, and it's a great thing: it means a guaranteed job when you finish.

Who offers bursaries?

  • Large companies — in mining, engineering, finance, IT, energy and more, often targeting scarce skills.
  • Government departments and state-owned entities — across many fields.
  • Universities and TVET colleges — their own merit and need-based bursaries.
  • Foundations and NGOs — often focused on specific groups or communities.

What you'll usually need

  • A certified copy of your ID
  • Your latest academic results (matric certificate or university transcript)
  • Proof of acceptance or registration at an institution
  • A motivational letter explaining why you deserve the bursary
  • Proof of household income (for need-based bursaries)
  • Sometimes a CV and references
Start a "bursary folder" now. Keep certified copies of your ID, results and proof of registration ready in one place. When a bursary opens with a short window, you'll be able to apply the same day instead of scrambling.

How to give yourself the best chance

1. Apply early and apply widely

Don't put all your hope in one bursary. Apply for several that match your field. Bursary seasons often peak between March and September for the following academic year — but each sponsor sets its own dates, so check constantly.

2. Match your field to the sponsor

A mining company funds engineering and geology; a bank funds finance and IT. Apply where your studies match what the sponsor needs — your odds are far higher.

3. Write a real motivational letter

This is where you stand out. Be specific: why this field, why this sponsor, what you've overcome, and what you'll contribute. Avoid copy-paste letters — selection committees read hundreds and can tell instantly. Keep it honest and personal.

4. Follow the instructions exactly

If they ask for certified copies, certify them. If they want everything in one PDF, do that. Missing or wrong documents are the most common reason good applications are rejected.

Watch out for scams: A legitimate bursary never asks you to pay an "application fee" to apply. If someone asks for money to "secure" a bursary, it's a scam. Real bursaries cost you nothing to apply for.

After you apply

Keep track of what you applied for and the closing dates. Some bursaries shortlist and interview, so be ready to explain your goals in person or online. And keep your marks up — most bursaries require you to maintain a certain academic standard to keep the funding each year.
