Nursing Jobs in the Public Sector: Registration, Grades and How to Apply

Provincial health departments advertise nursing posts every week. Knowing your category, your grade and exactly what the advert asks for makes the difference between being shortlisted and being overlooked.

In short

  • Public sector nursing posts are advertised by provincial health departments, often in the DPSA circular and on provincial websites.
  • You need current registration with the South African Nursing Council (SANC) in the category the post asks for.
  • Grades such as Grade 1 and Grade 2 depend mainly on years of experience after registration.
  • Specialty posts, such as theatre or critical care, need a recognised post-basic qualification in that specialty.

Nurses are the backbone of South Africa's public health system, and provincial health departments advertise nursing posts constantly: professional nurses in hospitals and clinics, specialty nurses in theatre and intensive care, operational managers, enrolled nurses and nursing auxiliaries. For a qualified nurse, the public sector offers stable employment, a structured career path and benefits such as medical aid, a pension and a housing allowance in permanent posts.

But nursing adverts are written in a very specific way. They refer to categories, grades, post-basic qualifications and years of experience counted from registration, and an application that misses one of these details may not be shortlisted. This guide explains what the terms mean, how to read a nursing advert, and how to apply in a way that gives you the best chance.

The nursing categories

South African nursing is organised into categories, and the South African Nursing Council (SANC) registers nurses in the category they qualified for. Adverts always say which category the post is for. In broad terms:

  • Professional nurse. A registered nurse with a four-year degree or diploma (or the newer qualifications that replaced earlier routes). Professional nurses have the widest scope of practice and fill most clinical posts in hospitals and clinics.
  • Enrolled nurse. A nurse with a shorter qualification who works under the supervision of professional nurses.
  • Enrolled nursing auxiliary. A nurse who provides basic nursing care, also under supervision.
  • Specialty nurse. A professional nurse with an additional, post-basic qualification in a specialty such as operating theatre, critical care, midwifery, paediatrics, psychiatry or primary health care.

Nursing qualifications have been restructured over the years, so your exact title depends on when and how you qualified. What matters for applications is the category you are registered in with SANC, and that it matches the category in the advert.

SANC registration: the first thing panels check

Nursing is a regulated profession. To practise, you must be registered with SANC and keep your registration current by paying the annual fee. Almost every public sector nursing advert lists current registration as a requirement, and many ask for proof of it.

  • Keep your registration certificate and your current annual practising certificate or proof of payment in a safe place, with scanned copies.
  • If you have a post-basic qualification, make sure it is registered with SANC too. The advert may ask for proof.
  • Pay your annual fees on time. Lapsed registration can make you ineligible for appointment, even if you were shortlisted.
Community serviceNewly qualified professional nurses in South Africa complete a year of compulsory community service in a public facility before they can register as professional nurses in their own right. Community service posts are allocated through a separate process, not through the normal weekly adverts.

What the grades mean

Public sector health professionals, including nurses, are paid under an Occupation Specific Dispensation (OSD), a salary system designed for their profession. Under OSD, many nursing posts are advertised in grades, usually Grade 1 and Grade 2, and sometimes Grade 3.

The grade depends mainly on years of appropriate experience after registration. An advert might say, for example, that Grade 1 requires a minimum number of years after registration as a professional nurse, and that Grade 2 requires more. For specialty posts, the advert may also say how many of those years must be in the specialty itself, after obtaining the specialty qualification.

When an advert lists two grades with two salary ranges, the department will decide your grade based on your verified experience. This is why accurate dates matter so much: your CV and Z83 must show clearly when you registered, where you have worked and for how long.

How to read a nursing advert

Nursing adverts in the circular follow the usual public service format, with some details that are specific to health.

Part of the advertWhat to look for
Post titleThe category, grade and often the specialty, such as "Professional Nurse: Specialty (Operating Theatre) Grade 1".
CentreThe hospital, clinic or district where the post is based. Some adverts list a ward or unit.
RequirementsThe basic qualification, registration category, any post-basic qualification, and years of experience for each grade.
DutiesThe clinical and management responsibilities, which tell you what the interview will cover.
Applications and notesWhere to send the application, which documents are needed, and conditions such as medical screening or verification of registration.

Pay particular attention to words like "advantage" or "recommendation". A requirement listed as "must have" is compulsory; an item described as an advantage is not, but it can help you stand out if you have it.

Applying step by step

  1. Find the posts that match your category and experience. Search the weekly DPSA circular and the provincial health department's own vacancy pages. Our circular guide explains how to read it.
  2. Check the grade you qualify for. Count your years of experience from your registration date. Apply for the grade that matches, or for both if the advert lists both and says it will grade you on appointment.
  3. Complete a current Z83 for the post. Quote the post title, centre and reference number. Answer every question and sign it. See our Z83 mistakes guide.
  4. Prepare a nursing CV with exact dates. List your qualifications, SANC registration dates, each post you have held, the facility and unit, and the dates you started and left. Include references who supervised your clinical work.
  5. Add proof of registration if asked. Include your SANC registration and current annual practising certificate, and any post-basic qualification registration, if the advert asks for them.
  6. Send it exactly where the advert says, in time. Hospital posts often go to the hospital itself rather than the provincial head office.

Preparing for a nursing interview

Nursing interviews are usually conducted by a panel that may include a nursing manager, an HR representative and other senior staff. Expect a mix of clinical, management and situational questions. Common areas include:

  • Clinical knowledge relevant to the unit: for example, infection prevention and control, managing deteriorating patients, maternal care protocols or theatre procedures.
  • Patient safety and quality, including how you handle adverse events and maintain standards.
  • Legal and ethical practice: patients' rights, confidentiality, consent and accurate record-keeping.
  • Teamwork and supervision, especially for operational manager posts, where you may be asked about managing staff, rosters and conflict.
  • Public service values, such as treating patients with respect and courtesy and providing services fairly.

Read the duties in the advert carefully and prepare a real example for each. Our guide to public sector interview questions explains how to structure your answers so the panel can score them easily.

Common reasons nurses are not shortlisted

  • Applying for a specialty post without the required post-basic qualification.
  • Not showing enough years of experience after registration for the grade.
  • Unclear or missing dates in the CV, which make the experience impossible to verify.
  • Lapsed or unproven SANC registration where the advert asked for proof.
  • Using an old Z83, or leaving parts of it blank.
  • Sending the application to head office when the advert asked for it to go to the hospital, or the other way round.

Questions people ask

Do I need to attach my SANC receipt to my application?

Many nursing adverts ask for proof of current registration with SANC, such as the annual practising certificate or proof of payment for the current year. Read the advert and the department's instructions: some want it with the application, while others ask for it only from shortlisted candidates.

What is the difference between Grade 1 and Grade 2?

Grades reflect experience. A Grade 1 professional nurse post is usually for nurses with fewer years of experience after registration, and Grade 2 for more experienced nurses. The advert states the years of experience needed for each grade, and those years usually count from the date of registration as a professional nurse.

Can I apply for a specialty post without the specialty qualification?

Usually not. Specialty posts, such as operating theatre, critical care, midwifery, paediatrics or primary health care, normally require a post-basic qualification in that specialty, registered with SANC. Some adverts say the qualification is an advantage rather than a requirement, so read carefully.

Where are provincial nursing jobs advertised?

Many are advertised in the weekly DPSA Public Service Vacancy Circular. Some provinces also publish posts on their own health department websites or online systems. Hospital posts can be advertised for a single hospital, so look at the centre listed in each advert.
