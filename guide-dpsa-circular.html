How to Find and Apply for Government Jobs on the DPSA Vacancy Circular

Hundreds of government posts are advertised every week in one document that most job seekers never learn to read properly. Here is how the DPSA circular works and how to turn it into interviews.

In short

  • The circular is published by the Department of Public Service and Administration, usually weekly, and is free to download.
  • Each department has its own section (annexure) with its own posts, addresses and closing dates.
  • Every post lists a reference number, salary, centre, requirements, duties and exactly how to apply. Follow those details, not general habits.
  • Most posts need the current Z83 form and a CV. Only shortlisted candidates are usually asked for certified copies.

If you are looking for a job in the South African public service, the single most useful document you can learn to read is the Public Service Vacancy Circular. It is published by the Department of Public Service and Administration (DPSA) and brings together vacancies from national departments and many provincial administrations in one place. In a single week it can list hundreds of posts, from cleaners and admin clerks to engineers, nurses, lecturers and senior managers.

Yet many job seekers find it confusing. It arrives as a set of long PDF documents, each written in the formal style of a government notice, and every department seems to do things slightly differently. People give up, or they apply without noticing the one instruction that would have made their application count. This guide explains how the circular is put together, how to read a post properly, and how to apply in a way that gets you considered.

What the vacancy circular is

The vacancy circular is the government's official noticeboard for public service jobs. Departments send their vacancies to the DPSA, which publishes them together, normally once a week. Each edition has a number and a year, such as "Circular 36 of 2026". It is free to download from the DPSA website and you do not need to register or log in to read it.

Not every government job appears in the circular. Some provinces and departments also advertise on their own websites, in newspapers or through their own online systems, and state-owned companies such as Eskom and Transnet have their own careers portals. But the circular is the biggest single source of public service posts, and it is the place to start.

Free, alwaysThe circular, the Z83 form and applying for any government post are all free. If anyone asks you for money to send you circulars, "reserve" a post or speed up your application, it is a scam.

How the circular is laid out

Each circular is split into an introduction and a series of annexures. An annexure is simply a section for one department or provincial administration, and each is usually labelled with a letter: Annexure A, Annexure B, and so on. The introduction lists which departments are advertising and which annexure belongs to each one.

Inside each annexure, the department usually starts with general information that applies to all its posts: where and how to apply, any special instructions, and its employment equity statement. This part is easy to skip, and it is often where the most important instructions are hidden, such as whether the department only accepts online applications, or whether certified copies must be attached. Always read the department's introduction before you read its posts.

After the introduction come the posts themselves, grouped by level, with senior posts usually listed first.

How to read a single post

Every post in the circular follows roughly the same pattern. Once you know the headings, you can read any advert quickly and pick out what matters.

HeadingWhat it tells you
POSTThe post number in this circular and the job title, for example "POST 36/45: ADMINISTRATION CLERK".
SALARYThe yearly salary or salary range, often with the salary level, such as "R237,453 per annum (Level 5)". See our guide on reading government salary levels.
CENTREWhere the job is based: an office, hospital, college, town or region.
REQUIREMENTSThe minimum qualifications, experience, knowledge, skills and anything else you must have, such as a driver's licence or professional registration.
DUTIESWhat you will actually do in the job.
ENQUIRIESWho to contact with questions about the post.
APPLICATIONSExactly where and how to send your application: an email address, an online system, a postal address or a hand-delivery address.
NOTEExtra conditions, such as preference for certain groups, assessments or security clearance.
CLOSING DATEThe last day on which your application will be accepted.

Read the requirements slowly. Government shortlisting is usually done by checking each applicant against these requirements one by one. If the advert asks for "a National Diploma in Public Administration and two years' experience in a finance environment", an applicant with a different qualification or one year of experience is normally not shortlisted, however capable they are. Words like "advantageous" or "will be an added advantage" mean the item is not compulsory.

Reference numbers matter

Many posts have a reference number, sometimes shown next to the job title and sometimes inside the requirements or application details. You must quote it on your Z83 and, if you apply by email, usually in the subject line too. Departments receive applications for many posts at once, and the reference number is how your application reaches the right shortlisting panel.

How to apply, step by step

  1. Choose posts you genuinely qualify for. Compare your qualifications and experience with each requirement. Applying for posts where you clearly do not meet the minimum wastes your time and data.
  2. Download the current Z83 form. Use the new Z83 that came into use in 2021, available free on the DPSA website. Old versions are rejected. Our Z83 guide explains every section.
  3. Fill in the Z83 for this post only. Write the exact post title, reference number and department. Complete every section and sign it. Our guide to Z83 mistakes that get you disqualified covers the traps.
  4. Prepare a detailed CV. Include your qualifications with the institution and year, your work history with dates and duties, and contactable references.
  5. Check the document instructions. Many departments now ask only for the Z83 and CV at first, and request certified copies only from shortlisted candidates. Some still want copies upfront. Do exactly what that department's introduction and the post say.
  6. Send it the way the advert says. Use the exact email address, online system or physical address given for this post. Sending to the wrong address usually means your application is never seen.
  7. Apply before the closing date. If you are emailing, avoid the last few hours: large files can bounce or arrive late. If you are posting, allow plenty of time.
The circular rewards people who follow instructions exactly. Treat every word under "Applications" and "Note" as part of the test.

Understanding the extra notes

The "Note" section at the end of each post, and the department's general introduction, often contain conditions that affect you after you apply. Common ones include:

  • Employment equity. Departments state which groups will be preferred in line with their employment equity plans, for example women or people with disabilities in certain posts. This is about preference, not exclusion: you can still apply.
  • Assessments. Shortlisted candidates may be asked to write a test, give a presentation or do a practical exercise, especially for technical or senior posts.
  • Security clearance and checks. Appointments are usually subject to checks on your criminal record, citizenship, qualifications, credit record for finance posts, and previous employment.
  • Senior Management Service requirements. Senior posts often require the Nyukela public service SMS pre-entry certificate before appointment, and an assessment of competencies.
  • The no-response rule. Most departments say that if you have not been contacted within a stated period, often three months after the closing date, your application was unsuccessful.

A practical weekly routine

Because the circular is published regularly and posts close within a few weeks, the job seekers who get interviews tend to treat it as a weekly routine rather than an occasional search. Here is a routine that works.

  • Keep a master CV and a filled-in Z83 template. Each week you only change the post details, reference number and date, and sign a fresh form. This saves hours and avoids mistakes.
  • Search by your qualification, not just by job title. The same skills appear under different titles in different departments. An "Administration Clerk" in one department is a "Data Capturer" or "Registry Clerk" in another.
  • Keep a simple list of what you applied for. Note the circular number, post number, reference number, department and closing date. When a call comes for an interview, you will know exactly which job it is.
  • Read the duties before an interview. Save a copy of each advert you apply for. The duties are the best guide to what the panel will ask you.
  • Apply widely within your field. Many applicants hear back from only a small fraction of departments. Applying for every post you qualify for improves your odds.

Common reasons applications are not considered

Government shortlisting is strict, and many applications are set aside before anyone reads the CV in detail. The most common reasons are:

  • An old or incomplete Z83, or one that is not signed.
  • No reference number, or the wrong reference number.
  • Sending the application to the wrong department or address.
  • Not meeting a compulsory requirement, such as the qualification, years of experience, professional registration or driver's licence.
  • Missing documents that the department specifically asked for.
  • Applying after the closing date.
  • One application for several posts, instead of a separate application for each.

Questions people ask

Where do I download the DPSA vacancy circular?

The circular is published free on the DPSA website (dpsa.gov.za) under the Public Service Vacancy Circulars section. Each edition is numbered, for example 'Circular 36 of 2026', and is split into PDF annexures by department. You never have to pay for a circular, and you do not need to register anywhere to read it.

How often does a new circular come out?

New circulars are normally released weekly. Each one has its own closing dates, which are usually two to three weeks after publication, but every post can have a different date, so always check the closing date for the specific post you want.

Can I apply for more than one post in the same circular?

Yes. You may apply for as many posts as you qualify for, but each application must be separate, with its own Z83 that quotes that post's reference number. Sending one application for several posts usually means it is considered for none of them.

What does 'centre' mean in a government advert?

The centre is the place where the job is based, such as a specific office, hospital, college or town. It tells you where you will work if you are appointed. Some adverts list more than one centre for the same post, in which case you should state your preferred centre if the advert asks you to.

I never hear back from departments. Is that normal?

Unfortunately, yes. Most adverts say that if you have not heard back within a certain period, often three months after the closing date, you should take it that your application was unsuccessful. That is why it helps to apply for every post you qualify for, rather than waiting on one.
